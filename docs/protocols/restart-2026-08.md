## Phases

| Phase | Weeks | Defining constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Connective-tissue ramp | 1–3 | Every set stops **2 reps shy of failure**. No ladder advancement before week 3. |
| Build | 4–8 | Last set of each exercise may go to RIR 1. Ladder advancement per double-progression rule. |
| Checkpoint | 8 | Assess against the success metric. Re-write this doc. |

The ramp exists because tendon and ligament collagen turns over on a slower
clock than muscle. Muscle will accept load in week 2 that the elbow and
shoulder connective tissue cannot yet support — and rings are an unstable
implement, which raises that load further. Beginner enthusiasm plus a 245 lb
body plus rings is the exact recipe for a 6-week elbow tendinopathy. The ramp
is not caution. It is the thing that makes weeks 4–8 possible.

## Session structure

**Frequency:** 3 strength sessions per week, never on consecutive days.
Walking happens all 7 days regardless.

**Duration:** 35–45 minutes including warm-up.

Every session runs the same five slots in the same order. Only the vertical-pull
slot differs between A and B. Same movements every session is deliberate — at
this training age, frequency and skill repetition beat variety.

### Warm-up (every session, ~8 min)

Non-negotiable. At 44 with a 14-week layoff, this is where injuries don't happen.

1. 5 min treadmill walk, 3.0 mph, 3% incline (counts toward the step floor)
2. Band pull-aparts — 2 × 15
3. Scapular pull-ups (dead hang, shrug down, no elbow bend) — 2 × 8
4. Ring support hold (arms locked, rings at hip) — 2 × 15 s
5. Bodyweight split squat — 1 × 8 per leg

### Work (five slots)

| Slot | Session A | Session B | Sets | Rep range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Vertical pull | Band-assisted pull-ups | Pull-up negatives | see below | see below |
| 2. Horizontal pull | Ring rows | Ring rows | see below | 6–12 |
| 3. Push | Ring push-ups | Ring push-ups | see below | 6–12 |
| 4. Single leg | Split squats | Split squats | see below | 8–12 per leg |
| 5. Trunk | Hollow hold or plank | Ring support hold | 2 | 20–45 s |

Slot 1, session A: 3 sets, 3–6 reps.
Slot 1, session B: 3 sets of 3–5 negatives, 5 s lowering each.

**Set count by week:** week 1 → 2 working sets per slot. Weeks 2+ → 3 sets.

**Rest:** 2 min between sets. Rushing rest is how you convert a strength session
into a cardio session with worse form.

### Cool-down

10-minute walk. Counts toward the step floor.

## Progression rules

Each exercise has a **ladder** — a sequence of leverage rungs from easiest to
hardest. Load is never added by weight; it is added by moving one rung.

### Double progression (the only advancement rule)

1. Hit the **top** of the rep range on **all** working sets, at 2+ reps in
   reserve → advance one rung next session, and reset to the bottom of the range.
2. Cannot hit the **bottom** of the rep range on set 1 at 2+ reps in reserve →
   drop back one rung.
3. Anything in between → repeat the same rung, add one rep where you can.

**During weeks 1–3, rule 1 is suspended.** Rung advancement is not permitted
before week 3 no matter how easy it feels. Weeks 1–2 add reps only.

### Ladders

**Ring rows** — rung = torso angle, set by walking the feet forward.
1. Torso near vertical, feet under the anchor
2. Feet forward, torso ~60° from floor
3. Torso ~45°
4. Torso ~30°
5. Torso horizontal, heels on the floor
6. Torso horizontal, heels elevated on a box

**Ring push-ups** — rung = ring height and foot position.
1. Rings at chest height, standing lean
2. Rings at waist height
3. Rings at knee height
4. Rings just off the floor, feet on floor
5. Feet elevated 12"
6. Feet elevated 24"

Turn the rings out (palms forward) at the top of every rep from rung 3 onward.
That's the shoulder-health tax on ring pressing and it is worth paying.

**Band-assisted pull-ups** — rung = less assistance.
1. Heaviest band, both feet in
2. Heaviest band, one foot in
3. Medium band, both feet
4. Medium band, one foot
5. Light band, both feet
6. Light band, one foot
7. Unassisted

**Pull-up negatives** — rung = time under tension, then assistance.
1. Medium band, 3 s lowering
2. Medium band, 5 s
3. Light band, 5 s
4. Light band, 8 s
5. Unassisted, 5 s
6. Unassisted, 8 s

**Split squats** — rung = range of motion, then load.
1. Bodyweight, partial depth, hand on a wall
2. Bodyweight, full depth, no support
3. Rear foot elevated on a 6–8" step
4. Rear foot elevated + loaded backpack, 15 lb
5. Rear foot elevated + 25 lb
6. Add 10 lb per rung thereafter

### Never train to failure on rings

On an unstable implement, failure is a grip or shoulder event, not a muscular
one. The last rep should always be one you could have controlled. Split squats
and negatives are the only slots where a genuinely hard last rep is acceptable,
and only from week 4.

## Step floor

**12,000 steps per day. Seven days a week. Independent of everything else.**

This is the single highest-leverage rule in the protocol. It is what actually
moves body composition and cardiovascular health at this stage, and it was the
habit that broke on the trip. Lifting is the smaller lever.

Rules:

- **Intraday backstop.** Under 6,000 steps by 6 PM local → treadmill session
  that evening. 30 min at 3.0–3.3 mph is ~3,500–4,000 steps.
- **Weekly settlement.** 84,000 steps per week. A short day is repaid inside
  the same week, never carried into the next.
- **Repayment cap.** No single day above 20,000. At 245 lb, a heroic 25K
  makeover day buys a heel that hurts for a week.
- **Deloads do not touch this.** Walking is low-impact and aids recovery. Only
  strength deloads. Step floor holds through every deload week.
- **Treadmill counts.** So does incline. There is no purity requirement here.

## Nutrition

### Protein floor

**165 g per day. Floor, not target.**

That's roughly 0.94 g per pound of lean mass — enough to defend muscle in a
deficit, and low enough that it stays hittable on a bad day. A floor you clear
is worth more than a target you abandon.

Stretch: 190 g on training days.

Practical framing: front-load it. 50 g by noon makes 165 arithmetic instead of
a 9 PM emergency.

### Calories — moderate deficit

**Recommended: 2,500 kcal/day.**

Estimated maintenance is ~2,950 kcal (Mifflin-St Jeor BMR ≈ 2,120, lightly
active multiplier, and the 12K step floor is a real chunk of that). 2,500 is a
~450 kcal deficit — about 0.9 lb/week, roughly 0.4% of body weight. That is the
moderate end of the range that preserves lean mass and leaves enough recovery
capacity to actually adapt to the training.

> **Flag:** the system's current `daily_calories` target is **2,038**, set
> 2026-05-16. That is a ~900 kcal deficit, ~1.9 lb/week, and it is not a
> moderate deficit for a 44-year-old restarting resistance training. Held
> alongside a 12K step floor and three ring sessions a week, it will show up as
> stalled progression, suppressed HRV, and poor sleep before it shows up as
> faster fat loss. **Recommend raising to 2,500.** This doc assumes 2,500;
> the target in the app has not been changed.

### Adjustment rule

Judge by the **7-day rolling average** of morning weight. Never by a single
morning — daily weight noise at this body size is ±3 lb on water alone.

- 7-day average flat for **3 consecutive weeks** → subtract 150 kcal.
- Losing more than **1.5 lb/week averaged over 2 weeks** → add 150 kcal. Faster
  is not better; past ~1% of body weight per week, the extra loss is
  disproportionately lean mass and recovery.
- Never below 2,200 kcal on this protocol. If progress stalls at 2,200, the
  answer is more steps, not fewer calories.

### Hydration

112 oz/day. Existing target, unchanged.

## Deload trigger

Deload when **any one** of these fires:

1. **HRV** — 7-day average is 15% or more below the 30-day average, on two
   consecutive days. *(The system already computes this; `anomaly_flag` uses a
   15% threshold.)*
2. **Sleep** — under 6 hours for 3 consecutive nights.
3. **Performance** — reps drop at the same ladder rung across two consecutive
   sessions for the same exercise.
4. **Joint pain** — elbow, shoulder, or knee pain that is still present 48
   hours after a session. Self-reported.
5. **Skipped for fatigue** — a session missed for fatigue reasons (not
   scheduling) in each of two consecutive weeks.

### What a deload is

One week, and one week only:

- Keep all 3 sessions. Do not skip. Showing up is the habit; intensity is the
  variable.
- **2 working sets** per slot instead of 3.
- **Drop one rung** on every ladder.
- Every set at 3+ reps in reserve.
- **Step floor unchanged** at 12,000.
- **Protein floor unchanged** at 165 g.
- **Calories to maintenance** (~2,950) for the week. A deficit during a deload
  defeats the deload.

Resume the following week at the pre-deload rung, not the deload rung.

If trigger 4 (joint pain) fires twice in eight weeks for the same joint, that is
not a deload problem. Stop guessing and see someone.

## Success metric

**The primary metric in weeks 6–8 is logging consistency. Not a weight number.**

A day counts as **logged** when all three are true:

- at least one food entry
- a body-weight entry
- steps recorded

**Target: 6 of 7 logged days per week, in weeks 6, 7, and 8.**

Secondary, in order:

1. 3 strength sessions completed in at least 6 of the 8 weeks.
2. Step floor met on 5+ days per week.
3. Ladder rung advanced on at least three of the five slots by week 8.

**Weight is a lagging indicator and is explicitly not the metric.** At a ~450
kcal deficit, eight weeks is ~7 lb — well inside the noise band of a body that
carries 245 lb and just resumed training. Glycogen and water repletion from
restarting resistance work will mask real fat loss for the first 2–3 weeks. The
scale is data for the adjustment rule above and nothing else.

### Honest read on the retreat checkpoint

An unassisted pull-up in 8 weeks at 245 lb, from a 14-week layoff, is not a
realistic target. It is a 6-to-12-month goal, and the fastest route to it is
mostly the weight coming down.

The realistic week-8 win is: **one rung less band assistance, and a controlled
8-second unassisted negative.** That is a genuine, visible strength change, and
it is the thing that actually predicts the first rep.
